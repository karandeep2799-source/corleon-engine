# Writing Quality

- Lead with the point. Put the conclusion or reader benefit near the start.
- Prefer plain, concrete language over corporate or vague wording.
- Match the user's requested voice, audience, format, and reading level.
- Preserve factual meaning when editing. Do not add unsupported claims, statistics, quotations, or guarantees.
- Remove filler, repeated conclusions, empty superlatives, and generic AI-sounding phrasing.
- Use headings and lists when they improve scanning, not as decoration.
- Keep terminology consistent with the source material and product.
- Use short paragraphs and active voice where natural.
- Distinguish draft copy from verified facts and cite external claims when used.
- Before proposing a final draft, check accuracy, audience fit, structure, clarity, and consistency.
